Its preceding vector-zero and adjacent `B+0x2e` halfword stores do not change that final value. This proves the immediate post-store local bytes only.

WP-257’s helper description needs a register-target qualification. At helper entry, `r4` is copied from `r0` (the caller passes B), but the helper then executes opaque `BLX r2` at `0x2fc5c8`. On the local branch where post-call `r0 != r6`, it executes `STRH #3,[r4+0x2c]` at `0x2fc5dc`; where equal, it branches around that instruction. The predicate is precisely the post-call comparison. `r6` was set to zero before the call, but the post-call value is unknown under this review’s no-ABI-preservation rule. Also, since `r4` is not reloaded after the opaque call, the store is syntactically based on post-call r4. It targets entry `B+0x2c` only if that callee preserves `r4` (or independently leaves it equal to B); this is not proven. Therefore the exact local instruction condition for writing literal 3 is verified, while a write of 3 to the entry block’s halfword is conditional and open.

The same caution applies to helper stores later in the equal branch: their addresses use r4 after the same opaque call. No other helper instruction is a direct `STRH` to `r4+0x2c`. This bounded static observation does not rule out writes by opaque callees through aliases, nor does it prove the later r4-relative destinations point into entry B. The `B+0x2e` load in the helper is a separate halfword.

For caller retention, the minimum missing evidence is: (1) callee memory effects at the first helper BLX and proof of whether post-call r4 remains B; (2) on the unequal comparison lane, whether the conditional helper store actually targets B; (3) all opaque call effects after initialization, especially callbacks loaded from B+0x14/+0x18 and calls passed B; (4) disjointness of caller entry r3 from byte range `[B+0x29,B+0x2d]` on lanes that issue 4-byte result stores; (5) evidence that the 52-byte WP167 copy uses this exact B after the relevant initialization and preserves bytes 44–45; and (6) same payload/node/manager/candidate-object identity and absence of intervening writes for the WP254/WP255 positional gate chain.
